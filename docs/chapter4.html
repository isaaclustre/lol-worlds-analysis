Conclusions

To find the most impactful player, we look at the players who stand out across Chapters 1, 2, and 3 and who are also the names that usually come up in the best-player debate. The scorecard below ranks each of them on four measures: lane dominance (Chapter 1), kill participation (Chapter 2), and the two win-rate measures from Chapter 3. The carry list ranks players by how much their team wins above expectation when they are ahead of their teammates, and the carried list does the same when they are behind them.

Chovy ranks 2nd in lane dominance, behind only Blaber, but 110th of 125 in standardized-shrunk kill participation. Compared with other mid laners, he takes part in far fewer kills. This fits his lane-centric style, as he is known for suffocating his lane opponent. However, applying constant pressure in his own lane leaves him with less time to apply pressure elsewhere on the map.

The question is whether that trade-off is worth it. In games where Chovy is ahead and his teammates are not, his team wins 63.6% of the time, which is +6.9 points above expected and 9th on the carry list. That suggests he is very good at converting his leads into wins.

In games where Chovy is behind and his teammates are ahead, his team wins 64.7% of the time, only +0.5 points above expected (24th on the carried list). That is a high win rate, but it is about what other teams achieve in the same situation, which points to strong teammates who can carry games when Chovy falls behind.

Zeus ranks 10th in lane dominance and 4th in kill participation. He wins his lane and also takes part in a large share of his team's kills, which largely points toward his individual skill. He is able to use his mechanics to dominate his lane opponent and create opportunities for kills. It may also reflect his teams tend to play around top lane. Zeus has often favored champions like Jayce, Gwen, and Aatrox, who absorb resources and carry in the late game.

He is also 3rd on the carry list, with a +8.2 point shrunk win rate above expected over 15 games. When Zeus is ahead and his teammates are not, his team wins far more often than expected. In our sample, that makes him one of the most effective players at turning a lead into a win.

The carried list tells a different story. When Zeus is behind and his teammates are ahead, his team wins 0.4 points less often than expected, which ranks him 105th. The gap is small, but there are two plausible readings: his team depends on his performances to win, or his performances depend on drafts built around him. The data cannot tell these apart.

Faker ranks 94th in lane dominance and 93rd in kill participation, so by these measures he is not a dominant laner or a high-participation mid laner.

Even so, Faker ranks 4th on the carry list (+8.2 points) and 1st on the carried list (+3.2 points). When he is ahead of his teammates, he closes out games, and when he is behind them, his team still wins more often than expected. One explanation is that Faker relies less on a dominant laning phase and plays around his teammates, which would make him adaptable: effective whether he is ahead or behind.

Faker also shares all of his games with his T1 teammates, so some of this may reflect the team and not Faker alone.

Who Is the Most Impactful Player at Worlds?

Even after this analysis, we cannot name the most impactful player with confidence. Three limits stand in the way.

1

Team effect

Many of the top-ranked players are on elite teams: the T1 players who three-peated, or Chovy and Gen.G, who have won 6 LCK titles. Players who share most of their games receive almost the same credit in the win-rate measures, so individual impact is hard to separate from team strength.

2

Small samples

The players in the top ten of the carry list have between 6 and 24 games in that situation. Shrinkage reduces the noise, but small gaps between players should not be over-read.

3

What the data cannot see

Keria is the clearest example. His numbers are solid but lead no category, yet he is often called the most impactful player on one of the greatest teams of all time due to his macro knowledge, drafting, and game-winning playmaking. None of these appear in gold, kp, or win-rate data. Generally, this trend seems to hold for all support players.

Going by the statistics alone, Zeus has the strongest overall case: he wins his lane, takes part in many kills relative to other top laners, and consistently converts his leads into wins. His weakest measure is the carried list 105th, and his results carry the same team-effect caveat as everyone else's.
