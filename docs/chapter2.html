Chapter 2: Kill Participation

Kill Participation is a metric that quantifies how active a player is on the map and throughout the game.

Before proceeding with the analysis, we should address two issues we may come across and how they are remedied.

Adjusting Kill Participation

A kill participation percentage hides how many kills a player had the chance to take part in. Consider two games from the jungler's point of view:

Game A · 2 team kills @ 20 min
100% KP
vs
Game B · 10 team kills @ 20 min
90% KP

player took part    player not involved

Taking part in 9 out of 10 kills is stronger evidence of an active jungler than taking part in 2 out of 2. A fair measure should reward the player in Game B more. We do this in two steps.

1 · Pool→ 2 · Shrink→ 3 · Standardize
1

Pooling

ProblemAveraging per-game percentages gives a 2-kill game the same weight as a 10-kill game.

FixSum up an individual's takedowns and divide by total team kills across all games played.

\[ \text{KP}_i^{\text{pool}} \;=\; \frac{\sum_g P_{i,g}}{\sum_g T_{i,g}} \]

Games A and B: the simple average of 100% and 90% is 95.0%. Pooled, it is (2 + 9) / (2 + 10) = 91.7%.

2

Shrinkage

ProblemPlayers with a small sample of games may have noisy data that's pulled toward extremeties.

FixBlend the player's rate with their role's average. The more kills available, the more we trust the player's own rate.

\[ \widehat{\text{KP}}_i \;=\; \frac{P_i + \kappa\,\mu_r}{T_i + \kappa} \]

Illustration with \(\mu_r = 0.70\) and \(\kappa = 10\): Game A's 2 of 2 becomes (2 + 7) / (2 + 10) = 75.0%, and Game B's 9 of 10 becomes (9 + 7) / (10 + 10) = 80.0%. The order flips.

3

Standardization

ProblemRaw rates tend to favor high-involvement roles, as roles like support tend to get very high kp compared to roles like top.

FixMeasure each player against the others in their role, in standard deviations, as with the lane indices.

\[ z^{\text{KP}}_i \;=\; \frac{\widehat{\text{KP}}_i - \overline{\text{KP}}_r}{s_r} \]

Illustration with made-up numbers: a jungler at 80% (role mean 72%, \(s_r\) = 4 points) and a top laner at 72% (role mean 62%, \(s_r\) = 5 points) both score \(z = +2.0\).

Shrinkage is equivalent to a weighted blend of the player's pooled rate and the role average:

\[ \widehat{\text{KP}}_i \;=\; w_i\,\text{KP}_i^{\text{pool}} + (1 - w_i)\,\mu_r, \qquad w_i \;=\; \frac{T_i}{T_i + \kappa} \]

A player with few team kills available (small \(T_i\)) is pulled strongly toward the role average. A player with many keeps nearly their own rate.

Where:

  • \(P_{i,g}\) is player \(i\)'s kills plus assists in game \(g\), and \(P_i = \sum_g P_{i,g}\).
  • \(T_{i,g}\) is player \(i\)'s team kills in game \(g\) over the same window, and \(T_i = \sum_g T_{i,g}\).
  • \(\mu_r\) is the average kill participation for role \(r\), pooled over all of that role's team kills.
  • \(\kappa\) is the strength of the role average, measured in "pseudo-kills" (here, \(\kappa = [\text{fitted value}]\)). It is estimated from the data separately for each window.
  • \(w_i\) is the weight placed on the player's own pooled rate.
  • \(\overline{\text{KP}}_r\) and \(s_r\) are the mean and standard deviation of the shrunk rates \(\widehat{\text{KP}}\) across the players in role \(r\). This mean is unweighted, so it differs slightly from \(\mu_r\), which is weighted by team kills.
