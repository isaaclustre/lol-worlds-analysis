Part b. Outliers, Standardization, and Ranking Metrics

There are currently three problems with our current analysis.

  • Outliers: There are a handful of games where strategies are used that warp a player's laning statistics.
  • Standardization: Comparing laning statistics across roles is unfair, particularly for supports, who often don't get cs nor gold leads of the same magnitude as a mid laner, per se.
  • Ranking Metrics: The current analysis uses an ordinal ranking, which provides no information on the actual gap between player performances.

First, we address the outliers in our data.

i. Outliers

In addition to Ignar seemingly being one of the most lane dominant players at Worlds, his laning partner, FBI, is consistently one of the worst.

Upon inspection of NRG's games at Worlds, we see that 3 of their 8 games drafted Senna and Tahm Kench and employed a fasting Senna strategy.

ii. Standardization

Metrics like cs, gold, or xp difference are especially skewed towards roles like bot and mid, as teams tend to funnel resources toward their carries.

This is particularly biased against supports, who may be outplaying their opponent but only be slightly leading in cs, gold, or xp.

So, rather than looking at a player's raw gold, cs, or xp lead/deficit, we consider their role-standardized z-score.
In other words, we look at how many standard deviations they are away from their role-specific mean in each category.

Standardization Formula:
\[ z_{i,m} \;=\; \frac{\bar{x}_{i,m} - \mu_{r,m}}{\sigma_{r,m}} \]

iii. Ranking Metric

Previously, we had been ranking players based on the sum of their cs, gold, and xp diff ranks across the 10, 15, and 20 minute mark.

i.e. a player ranked 4th, 6th, and 3rd in cs, gold, and xp diff, respectively, would have a score of 4 + 6 + 3 = 13.
The lower the score, the higher their ranking.

Additionally, cs, gold, and xp difference are highly correlated metrics and coincide with one another.

So, we should consider a laning index that holistically describes how a player is performing throughout the game.

We will create two performance indices: Mean and PCA-Weighted Mean

The regular mean performance index finds the average score across each of the 9 metrics.

Mean Laning Index:
\[ \text{mean_performance_index}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{9} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} z_{i,m} = \frac{1}{9}\left( z_{i,\text{gold}@10} + z_{i,\text{gold}@15} + \cdots + z_{i,\text{xp}@20} \right) \]

The PCA laning index using principal component analysis to instead calculate a weighted average.

The weights are the loadings of the first principal component divided by the proportion of the variance explained by the first component (70.2%).

PCA Laning Index:
\[ \text{pca_performance_index}_i \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_1}} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{M}} w_m \, z_{i,m} \]
