Chapter 3: Win Rate Above Expected

While lane dominance and kill participation describes a player's activity, it doesn't reflect whether or not that activity is getting converted into wins. Lane dominance and kill participation describe what a player does.

This chapter asks two new questions:

  1. When a player has a lead, what is their new expected win rate?
  2. When a player is at a deficit, what is their new expected win rate?

To answer these questions we label whether a player and their team had a lead, deficit, or neither at 15 minutes. We then compare their win rates within each label by doing the following steps.

1 · States→ 2 · Wins above expected→ 3 · Game States→ 4 · Shrinkage

1. Defining Game States

Each game gets two game state labels: one for the individual player and another for their teammates To define a lead, we use gold difference at 15 minutes. Since a raw gold lead may mean different things for different roles, we instead use a role-standardized gold difference at 15 minutes. An individual / team has a lead if they are more than 0.4 standard deviations ahead, and have a deficit if it they are more than 0.4 behin. Otherwise, the game state is even.


State Definition:
\[ \text{state} \;=\; \begin{cases} \text{lead} & z \gt +0.4 \\ \text{deficit} & z \lt -0.4 \\ \text{even} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \qquad z \;=\; \frac{\text{GD}_{15}}{s_r} \]


Where:

  • \(\text{GD}_{15}\) is gold difference at 15 minutes against the direct opponent (for teammates, the combined gold difference of the other four players).
  • \(s_r\) is the standard deviation of that quantity for role \(r\). Gold difference averages to zero by construction (every lead has a direct correlating deficit), so we scale without centering.
  • The cutoff of 0.4 standard deviations is chosen due to the fact that moderate leads are quite substantial.

2. Wins Above Expected

Using the 3 individual game states and the 3 team game states, we can create 9 possible game states. We call each combination a cell. For each cell, we measure the overall win rate of that game state. We then calculate an individual's overall win rate in that game state. Wins above expected measures how far above / behind a player is from the expected win rate of that game state.

1

Wins Above Expected

IdeaWins above expected are the additional wins a player got in a certain game state compared to other players of the same role and in the same game state.

\[ \bar p_{-i,c} \;=\; \frac{W_c - w_{i,c}}{N_c - n_{i,c}} \] \[ \text{WAE}_i \;=\; \sum_{c} \left( w_{i,c} - n_{i,c}\,\bar p_{-i,c} \right) \]

We leave the individual player out to avoid comparing them with themself.

2

Win Rate Above Expected

IdeaWin rate above expected is the difference in win rate between a player in a particular gamestate, and the expected win rate of players of the same role and same game state.

\[ \text{WRAE}_i \;=\; \frac{\text{WAE}_i}{N_i} \] \[ \text{(single cell)}\quad \frac{w_{i,c}}{n_{i,c}} - \bar p_{-i,c} \]

A WRAE of +0.10 means the player's teams won 10 percentage points more often than expected in that game state.

Where:

  • A cell \(c\) is a position × player state × teammate state combination.
  • \(w_{i,c}\) and \(n_{i,c}\) are player \(i\)’s wins and games in cell \(c\). \(W_c\) and \(N_c\) are the wins and games of all players at that position in the cell.
  • \(N_i = \sum_c n_{i,c}\) is the player’s total games across cells.
  • If a player is the only one in a cell, there is no benchmark and the cell is skipped.

3. Game States

We are interested in an individual's game state. There are a few different situations a player can be in:

  1. Type 1 Carry: Individual Lead + Team Deficit/Even
  2. Type 2 Carry: Individual Even + Team Deficit
  3. Type 1 Carried: Individual Deficit + Team Even/Lead
  4. Type 2 Carried: Individual Even + Team Lead
  5. Aligned: Individual's Game State = Team Game State
A

Carry

SituationThe player is relatively ahead of their teammates.

QuestionWhen this player has the lead and their team does not, does the team win more than expected?

InterpretationHigh: the player is able to use their lead to close out games.
Low: the player is unable to convert leads into wins.

B

Carried

SituationThe player is relatively behind their teammates.

QuestionWhen the player is behind but their team is not, does the team win more or less than expected?

InterpretationHigh: the player is able to play weakside and let their team carry.
Low: the team's results suffer when they fall behind.

C

Aligned

SituationThe player and their teammates are in the same state: (Lead, Lead), (Even, Even), or (Deficit, Deficit).

QuestionWhen the player and their team are in the same situation, does the team win more than expected?

InterpretationHigh: the team beats the typical result for this situation.
Low: the team falls short of it. Because the player is no different from their teammates here, this mostly reflects the team and works as a baseline.

NOTE: These are association, not proof of cause: a team's late game, drafting, and coaching also show up in these win rates.

4. Shrunk Wins Above Expected

Players with small sample sizes in these situations have noisy win rates, and variance in performance can be misinterpreted as skill or the lack thereof. Shrinkage accounts for small sample sizes by pulling each player's rate toward zero in proportion to how little evidence it rests on. We add up a player's wins above expected across all of their cells and then shrink once, so a player is not shrunk harder just because their games landed in more cells.

The shrunk win rate above expected is a weighted version of the raw rate:

\[ \widehat{\text{WRAE}}_i \;=\; \frac{N_i}{N_i + k_i}\,\text{WRAE}_i \;=\; \frac{\text{WAE}_i}{N_i + k_i}, \qquad k_i \;=\; \frac{v_i}{\tau^{2}} \]

Multiplying by games played turns the rate back into shrunk wins above expected: \(\widehat{\text{WAE}}_i = N_i\,\widehat{\text{WRAE}}_i\).

Where:

  • \(\text{WAE}_i\) and \(N_i\) are the player’s wins above expected and games, summed across the cells in the situation being ranked (for example the carry or carried cells).
  • \(v_i\) is the average per-game variance of a win, \(\bar p(1-\bar p)\), over the player’s cells.
  • \(\tau^{2}\) is the variance of true player effects, estimated from the data (here \(\tau = [0.044]\) for the full ranking; [\(\tau_{\text{carry}}\)] and [\(\tau_{\text{carried}}\)] for the two off-diagonal situations).
  • \(k_i\) is the strength of the shrinkage, in “pseudo-games”. A player with \(N_i = k_i\) games keeps half of his own rate.
